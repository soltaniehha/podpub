When A Machine Claims The Kakeya Conjecture (2000–2026)

In this episode we unpack "Local smoothing for the Euclidean wave equation", a 59 page preprint credited simply to OpenAI and posted to its openai/math repository on 26 October 2026 with a Lean formalization. OpenAI says one of its internal models produced the proof, which claims to settle Christopher Sogge's 1991 local smoothing conjecture in every dimension and, with it, the Bochner–Riesz, Fourier restriction and Kakeya conjectures that have anchored harmonic analysis for decades. No outside mathematician has confirmed it yet.

We walk through what local smoothing asks about focusing waves, the chain of consequences the paper deduces (Bochner–Riesz means, Stein's square function, restriction for the sphere, paraboloid and cone, and full Hausdorff dimension for every Kakeya set), and the unusual method: a conserved current built from the wave system in two auxiliary time variables, fed into a quaternionic Monge–Ampère equation and an Aubin–Mabuchi style energy, in place of the wave-packet and decoupling machinery the field has relied on. We set it against Terence Tao's map of how Kakeya, restriction and waves connect, Bourgain and Demeter's decoupling theorem, Guth, Wang and Zhang's resolution of the two-dimensional case, and Wang and Zahl's proof of Kakeya in three dimensions, and close on what a Lean proof does and does not establish, and on how a landmark result with no human author gets checked.
