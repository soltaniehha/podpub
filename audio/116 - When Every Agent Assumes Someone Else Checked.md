Aligning AI Teams (November 2026)

In this episode we unpack a new preprint from Siddarth Srinivasan of Harvard, Morgan Jane Matthews of the Constellation Institute, and Jascha Sohl-Dickstein and Erik Jones of Anthropic, written through the Anthropic Fellows Program. The argument: a team built from individually aligned AI agents is not automatically an aligned team, and the main reason is an old human failure, diffusion of responsibility, where every agent assumes the system-level problem belongs to someone else.

We walk through the magelab harness the authors release, the project-manager-plus-eight-coders teams running on Claude Opus 4.1, Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.6, Opus 4.7 and Mythos Preview, and the deliberately infeasible sepsis-triage, news-recommender and consulting-proposal tasks that force a choice between safety and business goals. We cover the finding that the team-versus-single gap does not shrink with capability (Mythos Preview shows the largest gap on one task and the smallest on another), why group chats and reviewer agents mostly fail to close it, the mediation analysis tying the gap to agents that never test, never discover, or deflect the harm, and the fix that works best: a designated safety lead plus a system-level safety specifications document. We set it against the same group's earlier AI Organizations paper, Naumov and Tao's formal result that only an "elected dictatorship" avoids diffusion of responsibility, the MAST taxonomy of multi-agent failures, and the Cooperative AI Foundation's map of multi-agent risks, and close on what it means to evaluate the team rather than the model.
