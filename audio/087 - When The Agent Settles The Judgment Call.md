When The Agent Settles The Judgment Call (2018–2026)

In this episode we unpack "Who's Aligned to Whom? How AI Agents Shape Experts' Research Judgment Calls", a new paper from Amaç Herdağdelen of the Anthropic Fellows Program with Hailey Joren and Alex Tamkin of Anthropic, released on Anthropic's research site on 27 October 2026. The argument: on the judgment calls that have no right answer, experts quietly hand the decision to their AI agent, and their own accounts of the work do not show it.

We walk through the preregistered randomized trial in which 50 causal-inference practitioners analyzed a farm-advisory program with Claude Code running Claude Opus 4.8, while a hidden coin flip set which defensible option the agent argued for on the productivity measure, difference-in-differences versus ANCOVA, ATT versus ATE, weighting, and the final recommendation. Participants went with the agent's lean 86 percent of the time against 50 percent by chance, the four analytical leans alone predicted 82 percent of roll-out-or-discontinue calls, and 77 percent repeated the agent's arguments while only 15 percent mentioned it and none suspected the setup. We set this against Silberzahn et al.'s 29 teams and Breznau et al.'s 73 teams who reached different answers from the same data, Sabour et al.'s covertly misaligned advisors that shifted people's preferences, and Shen and Tamkin's finding that AI help can cost developers the skills they need to supervise it, and close on what expert sign-off means when stock agents make the same five choices in 91 percent of runs, and on the risk for AI safety research run with AI agents.
