When An AI Agent Takes On The Math Behind Encryption (1992–2026)

In this episode we unpack three papers that Google Research posted to GitHub on 25 November 2026, each credited entirely to Cogentic, a multi-agent proof-discovery harness built by Yang Cai, Vineet Gupta, Aranyak Mehta and colleagues and running on Gemini 4, with no human input beyond the opening prompt. Together they claim unconditional answers to old questions about how hard it is to factor integers and compute discrete logarithms, the problems that RSA and Diffie-Hellman rest on. They land in a week when Nature is reporting rumours that AI labs are sitting on encryption "bombshells". The proofs are long, no human mathematician has yet published a check of them, and the Lean formalizations they promise are not yet public.

We walk through Unconditional Integer Factorization in Time L_N[1/3, O(1)], which claims to prove the number field sieve's 1/3 exponent with no unproven smoothness hypotheses, beating the rigorous L[1/2] bound Lenstra and Pomerance set in 1992. Then Discrete Logarithms in Finite Fields of Fixed Characteristic in Sub-Quasi-Polynomial Time, which claims to push below the quasi-polynomial bound Kleinjung and Wesolowski proved in 2022. And Unconditional Deterministic Subexponential Integer Factoring and Quadratic Nonresidue Construction, which claims the first deterministic subexponential factoring algorithm, against a best known deterministic bound of N^(1/5), and nonresidues without the Riemann hypothesis. We set them against the Cogentic system paper's prove-and-verify loop and close on what these results do and do not mean. Large constants like 105 and small-characteristic fields keep them far from breaking deployed cryptography, and we ask how mathematics should check proofs that no person wrote.
