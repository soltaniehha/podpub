When The Paper Answers Back (2022-2026)

In this episode we unpack "Reimagining research papers as interactive and reliable AI agents", a Nature paper published on 16 September 2026 by Jiacheng Miao, Joe Davis, Yaohui Zhang, Jonathan Pritchard and James Zou of Stanford. The argument: a research paper should not be a static document that readers have to decode, install and debug before they can use it. Their system, Paper2Agent, turns a paper into an AI agent that acts like a virtual corresponding author, able to explain the work, reproduce its results and apply its methods to new data through plain language.

We walk through how Paper2Agent uses Claude Code agents to build a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server of validated tools, resources and prompts from a paper and its codebase, and how its AlphaGenome agent outperforms Claude with direct repository access and the Biomni agent, including a case where it questions a published causal gene call. We cover the Scanpy and TISSUE agents, the scale test in which 74 of 100 computational biology papers were agentified, and the psoriasis case where three paper agents combine to point at GPR137. We set it against the reproducibility problem measured by Trisovic and colleagues, the Paper2Code approach of generating code from papers, the Virtual Lab and Co-Scientist systems, and the security risks of MCP servers, and close on journal "agent availability" sections, maintenance, attribution, and keeping humans in the loop.
