StudentBench: AI and Human Tutoring Yield Equivalent GRE Learning Gains (September 2026)

In this episode we unpack a new study from Curtis Northcutt, Jonas Mueller and colleagues at Handshake AI Research, posted to arXiv on 23 September 2026. They randomly assigned 2,383 students to an hour with one of thirteen AI tutors, an hour with a live expert human tutor, or no tutoring at all, and tested them before and after on newly written GRE questions. The claim: AI tutoring and human tutoring produced statistically equivalent learning gains, and the cheapest AI tutor got there at a tiny fraction of the cost.

We walk through the equivalence test at the heart of the result, the tutors behind it (from Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5 Pro and Gemini 3.1 Pro down to the open-weight Gemma 4 31B), the finding that Gemma matched the human tutor at 918 times lower cost per percentage point of learning gain, and why equivalence held for Quantitative but not for Verbal. We cover the three StudentBench leaderboards for lesson planning, practice-problem design and conversational pedagogy, the way teaching style clusters by model family, and the surprising role of reply latency in driving engagement, practice and learning. We set it against Pardos and Bhandari on ChatGPT-written hints, Kestin's Harvard physics tutor, Stanford's Tutor CoPilot, the LearnLM and Eedi classroom trial, and Contractor and Reyes on whether AI-assisted learning lasts, and close on what the study cannot yet show: retention beyond one hour, and whether an AI tutor beats simply doing more practice.
