Artificial Intelligence: The Ambiguous Labor Market Impact of Automating Prediction (2019)

In this episode we unpack the Journal of Economic Perspectives paper by Ajay Agrawal, Joshua Gans and Avi Goldfarb of the University of Toronto. The argument: machine learning is a prediction technology, prediction only matters as an input to a decision, and so the effect of AI on jobs depends on whether cheap prediction replaces people, augments their decisions, or creates new ones.

We walk through the four direct effects on labor, the cases from Kira Systems and Blue J Legal to London taxi drivers, and the answer to Geoffrey Hinton's radiology forecast built on 29 O*NET tasks. We then test the prediction and judgment split against Human Decisions and Machine Predictions, Algorithmic Recommendations and Human Discretion, the radiologist experiment by Agarwal and colleagues, and the 2026 patent lawyer trial by Autor and colleagues, and close on why a human in the loop is not a design and how universities can teach judgment.

Reference: Agrawal, A., Gans, J., & Goldfarb, A. (2019). Artificial Intelligence: The Ambiguous Labor Market Impact of Automating Prediction. Journal of Economic Perspectives, 33(2), 31-50. https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.33.2.31
