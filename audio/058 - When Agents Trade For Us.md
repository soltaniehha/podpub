Project Swap: What Happens When Agents Trade For Us? (September 2026)

In this episode we unpack a new study from Anthropic's economics team, written by Zoë Hitzig, Sylvie Carr, Tess Cotter, Kevin Troy, Kyle Turman, Maxim Massenkoff and Peter McCrory and published on 24 September 2026. 201 Anthropic employees across six offices each brought a book to give away, told Claude in a five minute chat what they wanted to read, and sent a Claude agent onto a trading floor to haggle for them. The finding: the agents bargained well, and what held the market back was how little they understood about the people they represented.

We walk through the 61 percent pairwise agreement between Claude's inferred rankings and people's own, the decomposition showing that imprecise preferences explain 85 percent of the gap to the best possible allocation while decentralized trading explains the rest, the comparison with the Top Trading Cycles mechanism, and the reruns across Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.8 and Fable 5, where the model an agent ran on mattered far more than whether it was told to be ruthless or prosocial. We set it against Anthropic's earlier Project Deal marketplace, the NegotiationArena benchmark, the agent-to-agent consumer negotiation study by Zhu and colleagues, Shahidi, Horton and coauthors on the Coasean singularity, and Hadfield and Koh on an economy of AI agents, and close on what fiduciary duty, agent licensing, identity and market rules should look like when the agents on both sides of a deal are not equally capable.
