Generative AI at Work (April 2023; published February 2025)

In this episode we unpack the study behind the most repeated claim about AI at work, that it helps beginners most. Erik Brynjolfsson of Stanford with Danielle Li and Lindsey Raymond of MIT track the staggered rollout of a GPT-3 based chat assistant to 5,172 customer support agents at a Fortune 500 software firm, published in the Quarterly Journal of Economics, and find that it raises issues resolved per hour by 15 percent on average and by far more for the least skilled and least experienced agents, while the most skilled gain little.

We walk through the identification from training-session and seat-cap constraints, the 36 percent gain for the lowest skill quintile against small quality declines at the top, the experience curve where two-month agents perform like six-month veterans, durable learning visible during software outages, larger gains on moderately rare problems, convergence in how agents write, better customer sentiment, 25 percent fewer requests for a manager and lower attrition among new hires. We then test how it held up: Peng and colleagues' GitHub Copilot experiment, Noy and Zhang's ChatGPT writing experiment, Dell'Acqua and colleagues' jagged frontier study of BCG consultants, Otis and colleagues' Kenyan field experiment where AI advice helped strong entrepreneurs and hurt weak ones, and Humlum and Vestergaard's 2026 Danish evidence of no change yet in earnings or hours. We close on what happens to entry-level learning, and to the experts whose know-how trains the model, when AI can hand that know-how to beginners.
