Canaries in the Gold Mine: Early Productivity Gains from Artificial Intelligence Creating Organization Capital (August 2026)

In this episode we unpack an NBER working paper by Tania Babina, Alex He and Renhao Jiang of the University of Maryland that revisits the Solow paradox for artificial intelligence. The argument: firms that invested in AI saw no productivity gains from 2010 to 2018 but clear gains from 2018 to 2024, and the reason for the lag is that AI pays off by building organization capital, the durable, firm-specific know-how that has to accumulate before it shows up in the numbers.

We walk through the new measure of AI investment built from job postings and resumes across US public firms, which counts more than 300,000 AI-skilled workers by 2024, the long-difference estimates of 6.2 and 7.5 points more productivity growth over six years per standard deviation of AI investment, the instrument based on firms' early hiring ties to AI-strong universities, sales growth without employment growth, the shift toward advanced degrees and senior roles, and a new job-description measure showing that AI jobs are nearly twice as likely as other jobs to build organization capital. We set it against Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson's Productivity J-Curve, the Census Bureau's 2026 survey evidence from Bonney and colleagues that AI is diffusing wide but shallow, and Baslandze and colleagues' survey of nearly 750 executives that finds a productivity paradox of perceived gains outrunning measured ones, and close on why the payoff from AI depends on the people who turn a firm's own records and routines into working systems.
