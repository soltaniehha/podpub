The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False (October 2026) + A 2^{o(n)} Time Algorithm for 3-SAT (October 2026)

In this episode we unpack two papers that landed within hours of each other and make the same claim: an AI model has refuted the Exponential Time Hypothesis, the 2001 conjecture of Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi that 3-SAT needs exponential time. OpenAI's preprint, posted to its openai/math repository on 26 October 2026, and Anthropic's paper, credited to Claude and posted on Hexagon the next day, each describe a deterministic algorithm that counts the satisfying assignments of any k-CNF formula in subexponential time. Neither result has been independently verified. Anthropic adds that its result does not settle P versus NP and gives no practical speedup, and OpenAI that it does not give subexponential algorithms for NP problems in general.

We walk through the two methods side by side: OpenAI's algebraic route through sparsification, Tanner-code parity tests, low-degree replacement forms and polynomial interpolation, and Anthropic's tensor-network route, which merges and cuts a graph of clauses and rewrites its edges with a decomposition built from the eight-square identity of Degen and Graves. We cover what falls with ETH (SETH, Gap-ETH, #ETH and the hardness results Anthropic counts in more than 800 papers), why both papers derive a circuit lower bound for E^NP using Ryan Williams's link between faster SAT algorithms and lower bounds, and Anthropic's own estimate that its bound only beats brute force above 10^10 variables. We close on provenance and trust: Anthropic reports that its core algorithm was found on 16 October with 14 million output tokens and no human input, and that its Lean 4 formalization was written and reviewed by AI with no person yet known to have checked it, while Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams organize an outside review that they say is not a certification.
