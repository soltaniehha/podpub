Emergence of Polarization in Networks of Large Language Model Agents (October 2026)

In this episode we unpack a new Nature Communications paper from Jinghua Piao, Yong Li and colleagues at Tsinghua University, with Fernando Santos at the University of Amsterdam and James Evans at the University of Chicago and the Santa Fe Institute. They let a thousand AI agents, driven only by a language model, choose whom to talk to, argue about gun control, abortion and party politics, and change their minds, and the society they build polarizes on its own, sorting itself into like-minded camps with no rule telling it to.

We walk through the three-stage simulation run on GPT-3.5 and replicated with GPT-4o, ChatGLM, Llama-3 and DeepSeek-V3, the collapse of neutral opinion, the homophilic clustering and scale-free networks the agents grow for themselves, and patterns that match the echo chamber and backfire effects. We cover the left skew the authors trace to self-inconsistency in right-leaning agents and the self-regulation prompt, borrowed from Bandura, that balances it, then the intervention testbed, where nudging individuals toward open-mindedness beats rewiring the network. We set it against Mäs and Flache's argument-communication theory, Bail's Twitter bot experiment, Santurkar's OpinionQA, and Chuang's agents that drift toward consensus instead, and close on the validation problem: what simulated societies of AI agents can and cannot tell us about people, and about the multi-agent systems now being deployed.
