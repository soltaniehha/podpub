In this episode of the ODSC Ai X Podcast, guest host Gerry Wolfe speaks with Mohammad Soltaniehha, PhD, Clinical Assistant Professor at Boston University’s Questrom School of Business, about moving beyond chatbots to build AI systems that actually do things — from real-time translation to personal agents that research, organize information, and deliver customized audio briefings.

Mohammad shares how he built a context-aware live translation app during his travels, why reliable AI products require much more than prompting a general-purpose chatbot, and how he approaches validation, guardrails, security, and human-centered design. The conversation also explores KlickBox, his interface for personal AI agents, the growing accessibility of AI-powered software development, and why he believes some of AI’s greatest opportunities lie in creating capabilities that were previously impossible rather than simply automating existing jobs.

Key Topics Covered:
Moving beyond chatbots to AI systems designed around specific workflows and outcomes
Building a context-aware live translation app with voice-to-voice AI
Why conversational context, tone, idioms, and emotion matter in translation
Using back-translation and validation layers to catch incorrect or unsafe outputs
Why some AI workflows should be hard-coded instead of left fully autonomous
Designing AI applications that reduce cognitive load for the user
How AI coding agents are enabling non-software developers to build sophisticated applications
The difference between quickly generating a prototype and building a reliable, production-ready product
Privacy, API security, row-level security, and protecting personal data in agentic applications
KlickBox and the idea of creating a mobile interface for your own personal AI agents
