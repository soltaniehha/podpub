Skill Deprioritization: Reorganizing in the Age of Generative Artificial Intelligence (July 2026)

In this episode we unpack a new Management Science paper from Matteo Devigili, Erdem Dogukan Yilmaz, Vibha Gaba and Henrich Greve of INSEAD. The argument: people skills are not one safe harbor from AI. In the year after ChatGPT arrived, firms began asking for fewer of some human organizing skills and held steady on others.

We walk through the difference-in-differences design across 1,820 US public companies and about 11 million Lightcast job postings, and the organizing taxonomy of task division, task allocation, information provision, reward distribution and exception management. In more exposed firms, postings for monitoring and task division fell about 23 percent and coordination 21.3 percent, while conflict resolution, staffing and incentive design showed no significant change. We test that against Gulati and colleagues on seven million postings, Fleck and colleagues on what German employers say they would pay for social versus prompting skills, and a World Bank meta-analysis by Angel-Urdinola and colleagues on whether these skills can be trained. We close on the limits of job ads as evidence, the risk of hollowing out expertise, and what universities should teach if routine coordination is the part that goes.
