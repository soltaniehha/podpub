How Much Is an AI Token Worth? Scaling Laws for Wild AI-Generated Web Text (September 2026)

In this episode we unpack a new arXiv paper from Jenna Russell, Mohit Iyyer and colleagues at the University of Maryland and Pangram Labs, which asks what the rising tide of machine-written web pages does to the next generation of language models. They find that about 31 percent of quality-filtered web tokens were AI-generated by August 2026, up from roughly 10 percent two years earlier. After pretraining 800 models, they conclude that this "wild" AI text helps only models starved of human data, and that for properly trained models an added AI token is worth less than nothing.

We walk through the WildAI corpus of 96 million documents labeled with the Pangram 3.3.2 detector, the finding that the FineWeb and DCLM quality filters keep AI documents 2.3 and 9.8 times as often as human ones, and the 800-model sweep from 19.9 million to 973 million parameters. We cover the new scaling law, which adds a saturating benefit and a logarithmic harm term to Chinchilla and predicts larger models better than the laws of Hoffmann et al. and Shukor et al., and the practical claims: an unfiltered crawl needs 1.6 times the compute today and about 3 times by 2028, and repeating human text beats adding AI text. We set it against Chinchilla's compute-optimal rule, Muennighoff and colleagues' data-constrained scaling, Shumailov and colleagues' model collapse, Gerstgrasser and colleagues' case that accumulating real data averts collapse, and Dohmatob and colleagues' tale of tails, then close on the limits of sub-billion-parameter evidence, the conflict of a detector company measuring its own market, and what a half-synthetic web means for the value of human writing.
