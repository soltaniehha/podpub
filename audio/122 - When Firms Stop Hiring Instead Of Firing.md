Working with AI (November 2026)

In this episode we unpack "Working with AI", a technical report from the Anthropic Institute by Nathan Wilmers, Maxim Massenkoff and Peter McCrory, published on 23 November 2026 and built on 66 interviews at 58 US companies that are in the middle of adopting AI, from law firms and dental groups to hedge funds, a trucking company and AI-native startups. The argument: AI is not yet causing many layoffs, but people at two-thirds of the companies say they are hiring less because of it, many are cutting contractors (often offshore ones), and the biggest gains go to the firms that reorganize work around the technology instead of simply automating a workflow or handing out tools.

We walk through the nearly 300 AI applications the authors code by reported value and by how much organizational change they required, the finding that most of the value shows up as more output rather than lower cost, and the three adoption strategies the interviews reveal: top-down automation that runs into expert resistance and fears of surveillance, bottom-up experimentation that produces power users and "Claude grenades" but stalls at bottlenecks such as code review, and reorganization through broader roles, redeployment toward the tasks AI cannot do, and shared data infrastructure. We set it against Paul David's dynamo and the productivity J-curve of Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson, against the finding of Bloom, Garicano, Sadun and Van Reenen that information and communication technologies push decisions in opposite directions, and against two quantitative studies, Humlum and Vestergaard's Danish evidence of no change in earnings or hours and Borri, Cheremukhin, Liu and Tsyvinski's fall in junior job postings. We close on what a qualitative sample of one lab's own customers can and cannot tell us, whether "we're not firing, we're not hiring" is a pause or the first stage of displacement, and who bears the offshore contractor cuts.
