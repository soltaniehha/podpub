Does Generative AI Narrow Education-Based Productivity Gaps? Evidence from a Randomized Experiment (February 2026)

In this episode we unpack an NBER working paper by Guillermo Cruces of Universidad de San Andrés and Nottingham, with Diego Fernández Meijide, Sebastian Galiani of Tulane, Ramiro Gálvez and María Lombardi of Torcuato Di Tella, which asks whether generative AI shrinks the productivity gap between people with more and less formal education. The answer from a preregistered experiment with 1,174 Argentine adults: an AI assistant closes three quarters of the gap on a workplace problem-solving task, but much of the gap returns the moment the assistant is taken away.

We walk through the design, an incentivized business task of diagnosing a problem from a report and drafting an email to a boss, with or without a GPT-4.1 assistant, followed by a module with no AI. We cover the headline effects of 1.242 and 0.834 standard deviations for lower and higher education groups, the gap falling from 0.548 to 0.139, the 0.171 gain that lower-education participants keep without AI, why carry-over shows up only when heavy AI use comes with real effort, and what the chat logs show about higher-education users directing and editing the assistant while others paste its output whole. We set it against Cui and colleagues' field experiments with 4,867 developers at Microsoft, Accenture and a Fortune 100 firm, Daniotti and colleagues' Science study of 30 million GitHub commits where experienced programmers capture the gains, and Shen and Tamkin's experiments on how AI help can stunt skill formation, and close on what a degree certifies when AI can stand in for part of it.

Reference: Cruces, G., Fernández Meijide, D., Galiani, S., Gálvez, R. H., & Lombardi, M. (2026). Does Generative AI Narrow Education-Based Productivity Gaps? Evidence from a Randomized Experiment. NBER Working Paper 34851. https://www.nber.org/papers/w34851
