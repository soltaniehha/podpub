Retrofitting Language Models To Operate Over Bytes (October 2026)

In this episode we unpack a new Nature paper from Benjamin Minixhofer, Valentin Hofmann and colleagues at the Allen Institute for AI, the University of Cambridge, the University of Washington, Imperial College London and LMU Munich. Almost every large language model reads text as subword tokens, and that hidden step is why models struggle to count letters and why some languages cost more to use. Their argument is that byte-level models have stalled not because the idea is weak but because everyone trains them from scratch, and that an existing model can instead be converted to read raw bytes for less than one percent of a normal pretraining budget.

We walk through byteification and the models it produces, Bolmo 7B and 1B from Olmo, Bwen 8B from Qwen3 and Blama 8B from Llama 3, along with the latent tokenizer architecture with its mLSTM byte encoder, a boundary predictor that looks one byte ahead, and the two-stage training that first teaches the new byte layers to mimic the original model. We cover how close the byteified models come to their sources, the large gains on the CUTE and EXECUTE character benchmarks, the drop in math and first-try coding, the efficiency frontier where subword vocabularies hit a softmax wall, and the task arithmetic trick that carries instruction tuning across for free. We set it against Sennrich's byte-pair encoding, the token tax on non-English speakers, Meta's Byte Latent Transformer and the H-Net, and close on whether future frontier models could quietly drop the tokenizer.
