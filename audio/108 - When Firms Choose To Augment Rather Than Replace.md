The Microstructure of AI Diffusion: Evidence from Firms, Business Functions, and Worker Tasks (October 2026)

In this episode we unpack a U.S. Census Bureau working paper by Kathryn Bonney, Cory Breaux, Emin Dinlersoz, Lucia Foster, John Haltiwanger and Aditya Pande, built on a nationally representative AI survey of American businesses run from November 2025 to February 2026. The argument: so far most firms use AI to augment their workers rather than replace them, and AI-driven job cuts are rare, but the cuts that do happen sit in the largest firms and in those that deploy AI across whole business functions, and the intensity of task replacement is rising.

We walk through the Business Trends and Outlook Survey's AI supplement: 18 percent of firms using AI (32 percent weighted by employment), narrow use concentrated in sales, marketing and writing, both bottom-up and top-down adoption, 66 percent of firms with task effects that only augment, AI-driven headcount decreases in about 2 percent of AI users, 16 percent of users replacing software and equipment rather than people, and the finding that functional deployment, not worker use, goes with job cuts. Then we set it beside Anthropic's Economic Index report, where individual Claude conversations lean augmentative while business API traffic is about three-quarters automation, Ryan Stevens' Ramp spending data showing firms swapping freelance labor for AI at roughly 3 cents on the dollar, Granulo, Raff and Fuchs' PNAS experiments showing that seeing AI as labor-replacing erodes trust in democracy, and Jeffrey Allen's Federal Reserve note on why adoption estimates disagree. We close on whether augmentation is the stable outcome or an early phase, and what universities should teach the managers who will make that choice.
